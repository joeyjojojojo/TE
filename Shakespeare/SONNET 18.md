SONNET 18

Shall I compare thee to a summer's day?    
Thou art more lovely and more temperate:    
Rough winds do shake the darling buds of May,    
And summer's lease hath all too short a date:    
Sometime too hot the eye of heaven shines,    ·················· 5    
And often is his gold complexion dimm'd;    
And every fair from fair sometime declines,    
By chance or nature's changing course untrimm'd;    
But thy eternal summer shall not fade    
Nor lose possession of that fair thou owest;    ················ 10    
Nor shall Death brag thou wander'st in his shade,    
When in eternal lines to time thou growest:    
··· So long as men can breathe or eyes can see,    
··· So long lives this and this gives life to thee.    ········· 14    



用现代英语改写（Modern English Paraphrase）    

Shall I compare you to a summer’s day?    
You are more lovely and more mild.    
Rough winds shake the beautiful buds of May,    
And summer doesn’t last very long.    
Sometimes the sun shines too hot,    
And often its golden face is dimmed.    
Every beautiful thing eventually fades,    
Either by chance or by the course of nature.    
But your eternal beauty will never fade,    
Nor lose the charm that you possess.    
Death won’t be able to claim you,    
Because you will live forever in these lines of poetry.    
As long as people live and eyes can see,    
This poem will live—and it will give life to you.    



通俗现代口语版本（Contemporary Simplified English）    

Should I compare you to a summer day?    
You’re even more beautiful and gentle.    
Winds can ruin May’s flowers,    
And summer ends too quickly.    
The sun can be too hot,    
Or its light too dim.    
All beauty fades someday,    
Because of chance or nature’s changes.    
But your beauty will last forever,    
And you’ll never lose it.    
Death can’t take you away,    
Because you’ll live in this poem forever.    
As long as people live and can see,    
This poem lives — and keeps you alive.
